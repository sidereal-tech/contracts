The V1 pilot is retired, not remediated

Decision of 18 August: the mainnet pilot is written off rather than wound down. It matured on 9 August having proven what it existed to prove — five immutable contracts, real funds, a full lifecycle on mainnet — and V2 deploys at new addresses regardless. Its manifest is marked retired and the keeper skips it, so it will not report upkeep nobody intends to perform. That closes M1 and the November archival deadline as accepted rather than fixed: the maturity rate can no longer be improved, and roughly $10.70 is involved, 96% of it the deployer's own position on both sides.

The keeper fixes still matter, because two of the three bugs found while making it see that market were not mainnet-specific: a failed contract read scored as a satisfied invariant, and PT coverage was checked against the live rate rather than the settlement rate. Both applied to V2 markets too.

The one irreversible decision left

The fee needs a number, and only before the first V2 initialize. yield_fee_bps and fee_recipient are now config fields fixed at initialization, with no setter, a 20% ceiling enforced in the wasm, and the skim taken from the PT-senior-capped surplus. Deploy scripts default to 0 bps, so nothing charges anything until someone decides it should. Peer protocols sit around 3–5% of claimed yield. Everything else in this report can be revised later; this one closes at that transaction.

CriticalC1 · AMM

The curve prices asset-denominated PT face against raw SY shares

contracts/amm/src/lib.rs:799 — let total_asset = state.total_sy;

total_pt is PT face in asset units, but total_asset is assigned raw SY shares with no index conversion. The curve enforces a rate floor and decays toward 1 at maturity, so the pool converges to “1 PT face = 1 SY share” while the tokenizer redeems PT at face × WAD / R shares. Anyone can mint PT for 1/R shares and sell it to the pool for roughly 1/e. Profit is riskless whenever e < R.

Figures are the profit of a single optimal trade as a percentage of the pool's entire SY reserve, on the deployed configuration.

This is tracked as M6 in findings.md and marked “accepted for v1” — but the accepted bound is wrong in a way that matters. The stated bound is “30 days of accrual ≈ 0.66%”. The real break-even is e < R, where R is the SY wrapper's cumulative appreciation since its own inception, not the market's term. A wrapper reused across a series of maturities carries all prior accrual forward: after a year at 8%, the first 30-day market minted on it opens with a standing 2–7% arbitrage against LPs regardless of its own term. The bound is 1 − e/R.

Realized loss is zero so far only because no third party ever traded the mainnet pool. Seeding V2 liquidity before fixing this converts it from theoretical to realized.

CriticalC2 · V2 vault

The vault's exchange rate can be inflated by a plain token transfer

strategy-blend:355-362, :335-337 · sy-vault-v2:704-712 · strategy-interface:109-114

assets_of is idle_balance + supplied_assets, where idle_balance is the strategy contract's raw token balance. The vault derives its rate from that with no virtual-share offset, no dead shares, and no minimum liquidity. Any address can raise the exchange rate by sending underlying to the strategy with an ordinary transfer — no auth, no deposit call, no interaction with the vault at all.

First-depositor exploit · stroops

Attacker deposits 1 → owns the only share. Attacker transfers 4,999,999,999 directly to the strategy → rate becomes 5e27. Victim deposits 9,999,999,999 → receives 1 share (non-zero, so the shares <= 0 guard never fires). Attacker redeems → 7,499,999,999. Net +249.99 USDC on a 500.00 outlay; victim recovers ~250 of 1000.

min_sy_out is not a defense: preview_deposit divides by the same poisoned rate, and the SDK defaults the bound to 0n.

The vector reaches through the vault into the tokenizer. The PT-senior reservation is ceil(pt_supply × WAD / rate), so a higher rate shrinks it and releases escrowed SY to YT claimants. A YT holder can donate to inflate the rate, claim the released escrow, and redeem it for the donation back — leaving PT under-covered for the cost of gas. That is the #9 Insolvent failure mode re-entering through a door the design did not anticipate.

V1 is immune and V2 regressed. The V1 wrapper values only the bToken position; its underlying_balance exists as a separate function and is deliberately excluded from AUM. Removing the admin rate-setter closed one path to an attacker-influenced rate; counting idle balance opened another to the same place. Exposure today is testnet only.

High

Six findings

HighH1 · AMM

Both YT flash routes break once the SY rate exceeds ~1.005

contracts/amm/src/lib.rs:457-465 (sell) · :1008-1041 (buy solver)

The second half of C1. The flash routes do perform the face-to-share conversion, which is precisely why they are inconsistent with the index-blind curve.

Sell side: the route requires e > R·(1+fee). At the deployed anchor that threshold is R > 1.0053. Once the wrapper has accrued about half a percent, no one can ever sell YT, and the quote errors for every input. Not size-dependent. As maturity nears, the threshold falls further.

Buy side: the solver binary-searches assuming the feasible set is a prefix, but above R = 1 it is an interior interval. When the first probe lands below it, the search discards everything above and returns dust — while keeping the user's input.

sy_inyt_outSY usedKept by pool for nothing
1.000.00000030.00000010.9999999 (100%)
100.00920.00 (capped)67.7932.21 (32%)

The quote function runs the identical solver, so a client deriving min_yt_out from the quote passes its own check and still loses everything. The code comment anticipates non-monotonicity but concludes the result “can only be suboptimal for the buyer”; a total loss of principal is not suboptimality.

HighH2 · AMM

SY-in swaps keep the full input when the trade is liquidity-capped

contracts/amm/src/lib.rs:390, :432, :920, :882-904

The solver returns the largest pt_out affordable within sy_in, and execution only rejects the case where the cost exceeds the input. Nothing checks that the cost is close to it, and the surplus is absorbed into reserves as an LP donation. Independent of C1 — this reproduces at a rate of exactly 1.0.

The rate floor caps a single PT purchase at 7.65% of the PT reserve at market open, so the cap binds early and ordinary order sizes are affected:

AUDIT.md characterizes this as “dust accrues to LPs”. It is not dust and it is not bounded. min_pt_out does not defend against it, because the quote returns the same capped pt_out. The fix is a refund of the residual, not a tighter minimum.

HighH3 · AMM

One atomic trade-and-reverse sets the TWAP to an arbitrary value

contracts/amm/src/lib.rs:1121-1147 (sync_twap)

The accumulator blends the post-trade rate in with weight elapsed / window — but elapsed is the interval during which the pre-trade rate was actually in effect. A correct accumulator weights the previous rate over the elapsed interval. Compounding it, a zero elapsed time returns early, so a same-ledger reversal is free, and the warm-up re-entry does not fire one second below the window.

Attack · 1800s window

Wait until 1799s after the last trade — an idle market is the normal case. In one transaction: swap 100 PT for SY, moving spot implied APY from 764 to 1748 bps at weight 0.99944, so the TWAP becomes ≈1747 bps and twap_warming_up() stays false. Then reverse at the same timestamp: elapsed is zero, the TWAP is untouched, spot is restored. Cost: 1.01 SY on a 1000-SY pool — 0.10%. 300 PT reaches 3736 bps for 0.8%.

The architecture document markets this TWAP to external lending protocols as manipulation-resistant collateral pricing, citing the February 2026 YieldBlox oracle exploit. At these costs that claim does not hold. Fixing it needs a real cumulative-price accumulator, not a patch to the zero-elapsed branch.

HighH4 · YT token

A YT transfer during a rate dip re-opens an already-paid yield interval

contracts/yt-token/src/lib.rs:438-447, specifically :441-444

let last = match Self::read_checkpoint(env, holder) {
    Some(c) => c,
    None => { Self::write_checkpoint(env, holder, rate); return; }
};
if rate <= last { return; }   // high-water hold, correct for existing holders

The checkpoint is correctly held as a high-water mark for an existing holder on a dip, but a receiver with no checkpoint is initialized to the current, dipped rate. Transferring YT to a fresh address during a dip resets that YT's high-water mark downward and makes the sender's already-paid interval claimable a second time.

Reproduced against the integration harness

Alice splits at rate 1.00; rate rises to 1.10; she claims 90,909,090 and her checkpoint advances. Rate regresses to 1.05 and Carol splits a fresh, fully-funded position. Alice transfers her paid-up YT to a fresh address she controls, writing checkpoint 1.05. The rate merely returns to 1.10 — no new yield beyond what Alice was already paid — and two claims of 43,290,042 now stand against a surplus of 43,290,044. Bob claims first and takes it; Carol receives 2 stroops, and after PT redemption drains escrow her later claim returns 1. Carol's entire entitlement transfers to Bob.

The repo's own invariant fails on the same setup: escrow 2047619048 must cover PT+YT claims 2095238091. It is self-profitable and repeatable — claim at a peak, wait for a dip, self-transfer, claim again on recovery. The random-sequence test misses it because that test only ever bumps the rate upward, and M7 below shows the dip trigger is real rather than hypothetical.

HighH5 · strategy-blend

max_withdraw counts idle underlying that withdraw can never deliver

contracts/strategy-blend/src/lib.rs:153-167 vs :209-244

max_withdraw returns idle + min(supplied, pool_cash) and its comment asserts that idle underlying is always realizable — but withdraw only measures the idle delta around the upstream call, so pre-existing idle is never paid out and is unreachable by any entrypoint. A strategy holding 100 USDC idle with a zero Blend position advertises it as realizable while every withdrawal reverts. There is no sweep and no rescue. Fixing this also turns any C2 donation from stranded-and-rate-poisoning into harmless-but-recoverable.

HighH6 · V2 vault

V2 burns the full share amount on a short delivery; V1 burned only what was delivered

sy-vault-v2:395-417 vs sy-wrapper:571-584

V1 recomputes the burn from what actually arrived when a fill comes up short, so a partial withdrawal consumes only the SY it paid for. V2 burns the whole requested amount and accepts whatever arrives so long as it clears min_underlying_out. With the SDK's default of zero, a user redeeming 60 SY against a 50-USDC-realizable position burns 60 SY for 50 USDC, gifting the difference to remaining holders.

The code comment claims a short delivery “reverts the whole transaction, so there is no state in which SY is burned without the matching underlying arriving.” That holds only when the minimum is set tightly, which neither the SDK default nor the trait-level redeem does. The V1/V2 equivalence test misses it because it only exercises fully satisfiable redemptions.

MediumM1 · Tokenizer

The maturity freeze pins the last pre-maturity observation, so PT is paid above face

contracts/tokenizer/src/lib.rs:565-583, :124-129

PT redeems shares valued at the frozen rate but worth the live rate, so a stale freeze hands PT more than its face, funded out of YT's yield. The exposure is the unobserved tail — and PT has no rate path at all, so PT↔SY swaps, the primary market, never refresh the observation. This is the finding measured live above.

MediumM2 · Operations

The keeper structurally cannot see the mainnet market

scripts/keeper.mjs:95-111, :224-236 · deployments/markets/

The keeper walks deployments/markets/<network>/, which contains only the two testnet markets. The mainnet market still lives in the legacy flat manifest, so the only automation that calls observe_rate and freeze_maturity_rate has never looked at it — the root cause of M1. Its TTL duty also calls sy.touch(), which exists only on the V2 vault, not the deployed V1 wrapper.

The same failure hit testnet: the short market matured on 16 August and is also unfrozen. That was the explicit dated gate in the V2 plan — “the keeper runs unattended across the 2026-08-16 maturity and freezes the rate without human intervention.” Nothing schedules it; CI has no cron.

Measured TTL: the vault instance entry and Blend's positions entry expire at ledgers 65,488,004 and 65,489,595 — both around 11 November 2026. The manifest's “no renewal needed for a one-shot cycle” holds only if funds exit first. Funds aren't lost if they lapse, but the app offers no restore path.

The flag excludes untracked files from the dirty check, so the entire V2 contract source — all three crates, still untracked — sails through the “tracked source is dirty” gate. MediumM5 · Economics

There is no fee anywhere, and V2 is immutable, so the decision must precede deploy

amm:837, :967, :993 · sy-vault-v2:43-46, :143

The only fee in the protocol is the AMM's 10 bps swap fee, retained in reserves and reconciled into LP value. Every other path charges zero. Searching the whole repository for a treasury, fee recipient, protocol fee, or collector returns no hits in any contract, and there is no sweep or skim entrypoint. The live mainnet AMM config, read on-chain, is {admin, fee_bps: 10, initial_anchor, maturity, pt_token, scalar_root, sy_token, tokenizer, twap_window, yt_token} — there is no fee-recipient field to point anywhere.

The V2 vault “exposes no admin entrypoint at all”, and the code matches: admin is written once and never read again. No contract has an upgrade entrypoint. A fee cannot be retrofitted to a deployed V2 market; it must be compiled in and set at initialize. Since the AMM is being redeployed anyway for C1, that redeploy is the free window.

The clean insertion point is the tokenizer's yield claim, skimming from pay — the value after the PT-senior cap:

let pay = if owed < surplus { owed } else { surplus };   // existing :394
let fee = mul_div_floor(&env, pay, config.yield_fee_bps, BPS)?;
consume_yt(&env, &config.yt_token, &holder, pay);        // consume gross
push_token(&env, &config.sy_token, &holder, pay - fee);
push_token(&env, &config.sy_token, &config.fee_recipient, fee);

Because pay is already the junior surplus, the fee structurally cannot reach the PT reservation — PT stays senior to the protocol fee for free. SY moves between escrow and the recipient without changing total assets or total supply, so the derived rate is provably untouched. Roughly fifteen lines. Taking it from owed instead would make the protocol compete with YT holders during a shortfall.

Do not implement a yield fee as periodic share dilution or an asset skim: both step the exchange rate down, and the tokenizer reads a rate decline as a strategy slash. And keep the sizing in perspective — at $1M TVL and 8% APY a 3–5% performance fee earns roughly $2.4–4k a year, which C1 leaks in a single arbitrage.

MediumM7 · Blend adapter

The “rate is monotonic” invariant is asserted in comments and tests but is false

contracts/blend-adapter/src/lib.rs:136-139, test at :196-206

The comment claims a Blend position's value “only rises with accrued interest… no admin can lower it, which is the whole point”, and the monotonicity test is documented as the property that makes #9 impossible. But Blend v2 socializes bad debt by decreasing b_rate, reachable through a permissionless pool entrypoint that fires when the backstop is drained, and it hits plain supply positions too.

The wrapper itself handles a decline correctly and stays solvent. The risk is downstream — YT rejects a rate below its checkpoint and the AMM has its own floors, so a dip can halt claims and quotes. It is also the trigger that turns H4 from a rounding-notch leak into a large one.

Also at medium: M4, re-using an address forfeits yield on newly acquired YT (the mirror of H4, same one-line fix); M6, the YT routes charge the fee on leveraged PT face, so a “10 bps” pool takes 1.6–5% of a YT buyer's capital while the docs advertise 0.3%; M8, SY is a live mainnet token that is not SEP-41 conformant and emits no events at all, so wallets and explorers see every balance as zero; M9, deposit_cap appears in every manifest and is read by no contract; M10, the two Blend-custody error codes are unmapped in the frontend, so a redemption failure renders as a bare number; and M11, the live admin is the transient deploy key rather than the mobile wallet the deployment plan designated.

Fix order

The sequence that was followed · steps 1–4 and 7 are done

  1. C2 and H5 together — same code area.

    Kill the donation vector and make idle payable in withdraw. Add the first-depositor and direct-donation tests the multi-strategy plan already names as required, before the fix, so it is proven rather than asserted.

  2. C1 and H1 together — one root cause, one fix.

    Value the SY reserve at the index inside the curve and convert outputs back to shares. Re-run the property suite at rates 1.0, 1.01, 1.05 and 1.1 across the plain legs and both YT routes; the existing 10,000-case suite is pinned where the defect vanishes. scripts/amm-curve-model.py is the bit-exact model behind the figures above.

  3. H4 and M4 — one line, plus a test arm.

    Initialize a receiver's checkpoint to the greater of the current rate and the sender's, or move to a global yield index. Add a rate-regression arm to the random-sequence test.

  4. H2, H3 and H6 — independent and individually cheap.

  5. Register mainnet with the keeper and schedule it.

    Teach it the V1 fallback and set a hard alarm at ledger 65,000,000 (~14 Oct) if funds will remain past October. Cleanest alternative: wind the mainnet market down — it matured nine days ago and holds $10.70, and redeeming closes M2, L10 and L11 outright.

  6. Decide the fee before the first V2 initialize.

    It is unmakeable afterward.

  7. Fix the provenance gate and commit the V2 seam so the manifests' claims become true.

  8. Reconcile the docs with the code.

    The YT fee rate, the “open” fee-distribution question that shipped a month ago, the TWAP manipulation-resistance claim, the missing zap function, the monotonicity claim, the TTL note, the recorded admin key, and a stale remediation checkbox.

Verified sound

Checked and clean — listed so they are not re-audited

The V1 admin is powerless. set_exchange_rate is permanently dead, gated on the pool being configured, and the live config reaches that gate. The pool can never be unset and re-initialization is blocked. The #9 Insolvent root cause is structurally fixed, not merely deprecated.

Reserve migration is a proven no-op. It takes no index parameter, re-derives from the pool, and accepts only the same underlying — and Blend v2 reserve indices are immutable, so the brick it exists to recover from cannot occur.

No accrual-timing theft on deposit. Blend accrues in memory on load without storing, and Soroban's ledger timestamp is constant within a transaction — verified empirically with two reads 78 seconds apart.

Illiquidity fails closed. A Blend shortfall is a hard revert, not a partial fill, and no pool status blocks a plain withdraw. Blend's admin cannot freeze the vault's exit, and plain supply is not seizable in a liquidation.

Tokenizer solvency holds under a non-decreasing rate. The PT-senior reservation is computed before payout, against a fresh rate, and rounds up so PT is never shorted by a notch. Split-then-recombine is strictly lossy in both directions, so tiny round trips cannot mint value.

Privileged entrypoints are properly gated. Mint, settle, consume and burn all require the tokenizer's own frame, which no external caller can produce. Every self-authorization is argument-pinned with empty sub-invocations.

AMM round trips always lose. Eleven sizes across two fee settings: every round trip loses at least a stroop, because both directions price the whole lot at the post-trade endpoint rate.

No quote/execute mismatch and no fee bypass. All four quotes call the identical helpers as execution, and synthesizing PT via split costs the same single fee as buying it directly.

LP mechanics are sound. Share invariants hold, first-LP inflation strictly loses money for the attacker, and post-maturity remove_liquidity deliberately still works — which matters now, since 96% of mainnet PT sits in the pool.

The V2 seam's stated rules are real where they aren't undermined by C2: no rate setter anywhere, measured deltas rather than requested amounts, immutable bindings, permissionless touch, argument-pinned auth trees, and rounding that favors the vault on both sides.

CI has a wasm float-opcode gate, globbed so new contracts are covered automatically, plus reproducible-build and registry-drift checks. It does not schedule the keeper.
